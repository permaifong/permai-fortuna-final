Kayap (shingles): is it contagious, what are the signs, and why the first 3 days matter

Kayap is the Malay name for shingles. It is painful, it is common after 50, and it responds best to treatment started early.

Permai Polyclinics FortunaJalan Penampang, Kota Kinabalu · 8 September 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

Kayap (shingles) is the chickenpox virus waking up again, often decades after you had chickenpox. It starts with burning or tingling pain on one side of the body, and a few days later a band of blisters appears on that same side. See a doctor as soon as you can, ideally within 3 days of the rash appearing: antiviral medicine works best when started early. You cannot catch kayap from another person, but someone who has never had chickenpox or the chickenpox vaccine can catch chickenpox from the blisters.

What causes kayap?

After you have chickenpox (cacar air), the virus never fully leaves. It sleeps in the nerves near your spine. Years later, often when your immunity dips, it can wake up and travel along one nerve to the skin. That is why the rash appears as a strip on one side of the body.

Anyone who has had chickenpox can get kayap. It is more common:

  • after the age of 50, and more so as you get older
  • in people with weak immunity, for example during cancer treatment, on long-term steroids, or with HIV
  • after a serious illness

The signs, day by day

  1. Before the rash (a few days): burning, tingling, itching or stabbing pain in one area of skin, on one side. Some people feel unwell, with a headache or fever. Before the rash shows, the pain can be mistaken for a pulled muscle, a heart problem or a kidney problem, depending on where it is. Do not assume chest pain is kayap. Call 999 if chest pain feels tight or squeezing, spreads to your arm, neck or jaw, or comes with sweating or breathlessness.
  2. The rash: red patches appear in the painful area, then turn into small blisters filled with fluid. They form a band or strip, most often around one side of the chest or waist, or on one side of the face.
  3. Crusting (7 to 10 days): the blisters dry and scab over.
  4. Healing (2 to 4 weeks): the scabs fall off. Some people are left with scars or patches of lighter or darker skin.

"If kayap closes the circle, you die." Is it true?

Many families in Malaysia have heard that if kayap wraps all the way round the body, it can kill. The shape of the rash does not decide this. Kayap usually follows one nerve, or two side by side, and each nerve covers only one side of the body. So the rash usually stops at the middle of your back and the middle of your chest, and a full circle is rare.

There is a real point hidden in the story. A rash on both sides, or spread over the whole body, is rare, and it does need a doctor quickly. It can point to a weak immune system, and in people with weak immunity kayap can spread to internal organs, which is serious. It can also be a different illness.

Is kayap contagious?

Kayap does not pass from person to person as kayap. The blisters carry the virus, though. Someone who has never had chickenpox or the chickenpox vaccine can catch chickenpox by touching the fluid or breathing in tiny particles from the blisters. Covering the rash lowers the risk but does not remove it.

  • Keep the rash covered with loose clothing or a light non-stick dressing.
  • Do not share towels, and wash your hands after touching the rash.
  • Until every blister has crusted over, stay away from pregnant women who have not had chickenpox, newborn babies, and anyone with weak immunity.
  • Stay off work if the rash is oozing and cannot be covered, until it has dried out. If it is covered and you feel well enough, you can usually work, unless your job puts you close to the people above. Ask your doctor if you are not sure.

Why the first 3 days matter

Antiviral tablets (such as acyclovir or valacyclovir) stop the virus from multiplying. They work best when started within 3 days of the rash appearing. Started early, they help the rash heal faster and ease the pain. The doctor can also give medicine for the pain, because the pain can be severe. If more than 3 days have passed, still see a doctor. Antiviral medicine may help less by then, but the doctor can treat the pain and check for complications.

The most common complication is nerve pain that lingers for months after the rash heals, called postherpetic neuralgia. It is more common in older people, especially after 50. Treatment as soon as possible can lower the risk, but it cannot rule it out. See a doctor if the pain does not get better after the rash has gone.

See a doctor the same day if:

  • the rash is anywhere on your face, especially your forehead, on or near your eye, or on your nose (it can damage the eye)
  • your eye is painful or red, or your vision changes (go straight away, to a hospital emergency department if you cannot see a doctor quickly)
  • you have a rash on or in the ear, earache, changes in your hearing, dizziness, or one side of your face feels weak or droops
  • you have weak immunity, or you are pregnant
  • you are breastfeeding and the rash is on your breast
  • you are under 18
  • the rash is on both sides of the body, or spreading widely
  • you have a high fever or a severe headache

Call 999 or go to a hospital emergency department if:

  • you are confused or very drowsy, have a fit, have trouble speaking, or an arm or leg turns weak
  • your face droops suddenly with arm weakness or slurred speech, because this can be a stroke

Looking after kayap at home

  • Keep the rash clean and dry. You can shower: wash the rash gently with water or a fragrance-free cleanser, then pat dry.
  • Wear loose cotton clothes.
  • A cool, damp cloth eases the itch. Once the blisters have scabbed over, calamine lotion can help too.
  • Do not burst the blisters, and do not cover them with tight plasters.
  • Avoid putting pastes, toothpaste, herbs or traditional mixtures on open blisters. Broken skin infects easily, and an infection makes scarring worse.

Kayap and cacar air (chickenpox): the difference

Cacar air (chickenpox)Kayap (shingles)
WhenFirst time you meet the virusThe same virus waking up years later
WhoMostly childrenMostly adults over 50
RashItchy spots all over the bodyPainful band on one side
SpreadVery contagious through the air and blistersThrough blister fluid, or tiny particles from the blisters, and only as chickenpox

Questions people ask

How long does kayap last?

The blisters crust in 7 to 10 days, and the skin heals in 2 to 4 weeks. Nerve pain can last longer, especially in older adults.

Can kayap come back?

Yes, though most people get it only once. It is more likely to come back in people with weak immunity.

Can I shower when I have kayap?

Yes. Wash with water or a fragrance-free cleanser, avoid scrubbing the rash, and pat the skin dry gently.

Can I catch kayap from someone who has it?

No. But if you have never had chickenpox or the chickenpox vaccine, you can catch chickenpox from the blisters.
