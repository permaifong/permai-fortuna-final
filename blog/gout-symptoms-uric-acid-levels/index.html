Uric acid normal range and gout: what triggers an attack, and how it is treated

A gout attack often comes at night, and the pain can wake you from sleep. Here is what to know, based on Malaysian Ministry of Health guidance.

Permai Polyclinics FortunaJalan Penampang, Kota Kinabalu · 1 September 2026 · 10 min read
The short answer

Gout is joint pain caused by uric acid crystals. It most often affects the big toe, midfoot and ankle. The joint turns red, hot and swollen, and the pain peaks within 24 hours. The normal uric acid range depends on the lab: for example, MedlinePlus (United States) lists 3.0 to 7.1 mg/dL for women and 4.0 to 8.6 mg/dL for men. The Malaysian guideline counts uric acid as high above 408 µmol/L (6.8 mg/dL), and the treatment target for people with gout is below 360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL), so a reading inside the normal range on a lab report can still be above these levels. A gout attack should be treated promptly, and the doctor chooses the medicine according to your other illnesses.

What a gout attack feels like

  • sudden joint pain that peaks within 24 hours
  • a joint that turns red, hot and swollen, and is hard to move
  • throbbing or burning pain, with the joint very tender to touch
  • it often comes at night and wakes you
  • sometimes tingling, mild discomfort or itching a few hours before the attack
  • sometimes a fever

The big toe is the most commonly affected joint, followed by the midfoot and ankle. Gout can also affect other joints in the feet and hands, and the wrists, elbows and knees. An attack usually settles by itself in 1 to 2 weeks, but the guideline advises treating it promptly because the pain is so severe. When an attack settles, that does not mean the gout has gone. If uric acid stays high, attacks can become more frequent and last longer, and the joint can be damaged.

A hot, swollen joint is not always gout. The guideline names joint infection (septic arthritis) as the key condition to tell apart from gout, and it needs to be checked by a doctor urgently.

What causes gout, and what triggers an attack

Gout happens when uric acid crystals (monosodium urate) collect in and around the joints. Long-term high uric acid is the main risk factor, but not everyone with high uric acid gets gout. Studies from a few specialist hospitals in Malaysia report that gout usually starts between the ages of 30 and 60, and that most patients are men.

Things that raise the risk:

  • being overweight
  • alcohol, sweet drinks, red meat and certain seafood
  • chronic kidney disease, high blood pressure and psoriasis
  • some medicines, especially diuretics (water pills) and some other blood pressure medicines
  • increasing age, being male, menopause and genetics

An attack can be set off by an acute illness, including one that needs surgery, by dehydration, by alcohol, and by high-purine foods from animal sources. Do not stop your blood pressure medicine on your own because of gout risk. The doctor will weigh the benefits and risks.

What your uric acid number means

Uric acid levelWhat it means
Above 408 µmol/L (6.8 mg/dL)High uric acid (hyperuricaemia), under the Malaysian guideline
Below 360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL)The treatment target for all people with gout
Below 300 µmol/L (5 mg/dL)A lower target for severe gout: tophi (lumps of crystals under the skin), chronic joint damage or frequent attacks

The normal range on your report depends on the lab. For example, MedlinePlus (United States) lists 3.0 to 7.1 mg/dL for women and 4.0 to 8.6 mg/dL for men, and says ranges can vary slightly between labs. MedlinePlus also says the target for people with gout is below 6 or 5 mg/dL, so a reading inside the lab's normal range does not mean you have reached the gout target. The Malaysian guideline does not recommend readings below 180 µmol/L (3 mg/dL) over the long term. If your report uses mmol/L, 360 µmol/L is the same as 0.36 mmol/L.

Two things to understand:

  • High uric acid alone is not a diagnosis of gout. The guideline also says there is not enough evidence to recommend uric-acid-lowering medicine for people with high uric acid and no symptoms.
  • A normal reading during an attack does not rule out gout. If the doctor still suspects gout, the test can be repeated two weeks or more after the attack has fully settled.

Gout medicine: what doctors use

During an attack

The guideline lists three groups of medicine: colchicine, anti-inflammatory medicines (NSAIDs or COX-2 inhibitors) and corticosteroids. The doctor may give one medicine, or combine them if one is not enough. The choice depends on your other illnesses, so no single medicine suits everyone.

NSAIDs such as ibuprofen can upset the stomach, raise blood pressure and cause swelling. They should be avoided if you have chronic kidney disease, and must not be taken if you are allergic to NSAIDs, have a stomach ulcer or stomach bleeding now, have ever had either because of an NSAID, or have severe liver disease, kidney disease or heart failure. The risk of stomach bleeding is higher if you also take aspirin, blood thinners or corticosteroids. Ask a doctor or pharmacist first, especially if you are pregnant, have other illnesses or take other medicines.

To ease the joint at home:

  • Rest the joint and raise the affected foot or hand.
  • Apply an ice pack wrapped in a cloth for up to 20 minutes at a time. Do not put ice directly on the skin.
  • Keep bedclothes off the joint at night.
  • Drink plenty of water, unless your doctor limits your fluids.

Long term

Uric-acid-lowering medicine is given if you have 2 or more attacks in 12 months, tophi, or joint damage from gout on an X-ray. The doctor may also consider it after a first attack if you have stage 3 or worse chronic kidney disease, kidney stones, or uric acid above 540 µmol/L (9 mg/dL).

  • Allopurinol is the first choice. It is started at a low dose and raised slowly until the target is reached.
  • If allopurinol is not suitable, the doctor may consider febuxostat or a uricosuric medicine.
  • Uric-acid-lowering medicine is continued during a gout attack. A rash or allergy is a different matter, as explained below.
  • Early in treatment, attacks can increase because crystals start to dissolve. That is why the guideline recommends steps to prevent attacks for at least 3 to 6 months: raising the dose in stages, giving colchicine alongside, or both.

Allopurinol can cause a rash. In rare cases it can cause a serious skin reaction that can be fatal. Stop taking allopurinol and get medical advice immediately if you get a rash, itching or other skin allergy, unexplained red eyes, or ulcers in the mouth or on the genitals.

If your doctor has put you on low-dose aspirin for a medical reason, do not stop it yourself, even though low-dose aspirin can raise the risk of attacks. The guideline advises continuing it, monitoring uric acid, and adjusting the dose of the uric-acid-lowering medicine to reach the target.

Also limit sugar, honey, cakes and kuih. Check food labels for high-fructose corn syrup. The leaflet also lists fish roe, tuna, kembung, ikan parang, tamban and mussels (kupang) as high in purines. It says that for the protein foods, beans and vegetables it lists to limit, what suits you depends on your own tolerance, and it suggests at least one serving a day of omega-3 rich fish.

Two ministry documents differ on some foods. The 2021 clinical guideline, written for doctors, says omega-3 rich fish such as ikan bilis, sardines, kembung and tamban, and plant foods such as spinach, mushrooms, legumes and oats, need no restriction. These fish are high in purines, but the guideline says the benefit for the heart outweighs the possible harm. If a doctor or dietitian has given you personal advice, follow it.

The guideline also recommends drinking at least 2 litres a day, and the leaflet mentions 2 to 3 litres of water. If your doctor limits your fluids, or you have heart or kidney disease, ask your doctor first. Lose weight if you are overweight, and follow the DASH diet, which means more vegetables, fruit and whole grains. Dairy products are linked to a lower risk of gout, and the DASH diet favours low-fat or fat-free choices. Cherries and vitamin C still need more evidence.

Gout often comes with other illnesses

The guideline recommends screening for high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, coronary heart disease, kidney disease and kidney stones when gout is diagnosed and during follow-up. Read our guides on normal blood pressure, blood sugar levels and cholesterol levels.

See a doctor urgently if:

  • a joint suddenly becomes painful and swollen, and you also have a high temperature, feel hot, cold or shivery, feel sick or cannot eat (this may be an infection inside the joint)
  • the pain is getting worse
  • you get a rash, itching or other skin allergy, unexplained red eyes, or ulcers in the mouth or on the genitals while taking allopurinol: stop taking it and get advice immediately

See a doctor as well if this is the first time you have had gout symptoms, or your usual treatment is not helping.

Questions people ask

How long does a gout attack last?

An attack usually settles by itself in 1 to 2 weeks, but the Malaysian guideline advises treating it promptly because the pain is so severe. When an attack settles, that does not mean the gout has gone. If uric acid stays high, attacks can become more frequent and last longer, and the joint can be damaged.

Can I eat chicken if I have gout?

Yes, in the advised portions. The Ministry of Health diet leaflet lists chicken among foods recommended in advised portions, and the DASH diet in the Malaysian guideline includes it too. Choose leaner parts and remove the skin.

Can gout go away for good?

Gout is a long-term disease. Attacks settle, but crystals remain between attacks. Treatment aims to bring uric acid below 360 µmol/L. If your doctor gives you uric-acid-lowering medicine, keep taking it even when you have no symptoms, because attacks often come back when the medicine is stopped.

Does drinking water help gout?

The Ministry of Health leaflet says drinking 2 to 3 litres of water a day encourages the body to pass uric acid in urine, and the Malaysian guideline recommends at least 2 litres of fluid a day. If your doctor limits your fluids, or you have heart or kidney disease, ask your doctor first.

Do I need to fast for a uric acid test?

MedlinePlus says you should not eat or drink for 4 hours before the test, unless you are told otherwise. Follow the instructions from your doctor or lab.
