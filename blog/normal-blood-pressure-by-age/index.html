What is a normal blood pressure? A simple guide by age

Nearly three in ten adults in Malaysia have high blood pressure, and many of them do not know it. Here is how to read your numbers.

Permai Polyclinics FortunaJalan Penampang, Kota Kinabalu · 6 October 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

For adults of every age, normal blood pressure is below 130/85 mmHg, and under 120/80 is ideal. A reading of 140/90 or higher at the clinic, found again on another day, means high blood pressure (hypertension). For the average of your home readings, the cut-off is lower: 135/85. Blood pressure often rises as people get older, but that makes high readings common in older people, not normal for them.

The numbers, from the Malaysian guideline

A reading has two numbers. The top one (systolic) is the pressure when your heart beats. The bottom one (diastolic) is the pressure between beats. Doctors in Malaysia use these categories from the national guideline on hypertension:

Does normal blood pressure change with age?

The cut-offs above are the same for every adult, from 18 to 80 and beyond. What changes with age is the target the doctor aims for once you are on treatment:

WhoTreatment target
Adults under 80below 140/90
Adults 80 and overbelow 150/90
Diabetes, kidney disease, heart disease or past strokeset by your doctor, for example below 140/80 in diabetes, and below 130/80 for some people with these conditions
Children under 13below the 90th percentile on charts by age, sex and height
Teenagers 13 and overbelow 130/80

Your own target may be different. A fit person aged 65 to 80 may be given a lower one, and a frail older person a less strict one. Follow the target your doctor gives you, and do not change your tablets because of this table.

The adult categories above do not apply to anyone under 18. Under 13, readings are checked against charts by age, sex and height. From 13, a reading of 130/80 or higher already counts as high blood pressure, which is lower than the adult cut-off of 140/90.

Why so many people do not know they have it

High blood pressure usually has no symptoms, often for years. You feel fine while it slowly strains your heart, kidneys, eyes and blood vessels. The National Health and Morbidity Survey 2023 found that 29.2% of Malaysian adults have high blood pressure, and 11.9% of all adults have it without knowing. In October 2026 the Health Minister said more than 80% of Malaysians aged 18 to 29 with diabetes or high blood pressure did not know they had it.

The only way to know is to measure it. The Malaysian guideline says every adult above 18 should have it checked at least once a year, and more often with a family history, obesity or an "at risk" reading. After a reading, it gives these times to check again:

  • Below 120/80: within a year.
  • Top number 120 to 139, or bottom number 80 to 89: within 3 to 6 months. The doctor also looks at your overall heart risk.
  • Top number 140 to 159, or bottom number 90 to 99: within 2 months, to confirm it.
  • Top number 160 to 179, or bottom number 100 to 109: within 1 month.
  • 180/110 or higher: see the box further down.

How to measure your blood pressure at home

  1. Use an automatic upper-arm monitor of a validated model, with a cuff that fits your arm. Wrist and finger monitors are not recommended.
  2. No food, coffee, tea, smoking or exercise for 30 minutes before.
  3. Empty your bladder, then sit quietly for 5 minutes with your back supported, feet flat on the floor and legs uncrossed.
  4. Rest your arm on a table so the cuff is at the level of your heart. Do not talk.
  5. Take two readings one minute apart and write both down.
  6. Do this every morning and evening for a week, at about the same time. Measure before your morning blood pressure tablets, if you take them, and before your evening meal. Then bring the list to the doctor.

Some people have high readings only at the clinic because they feel nervous. A week of home readings shows the doctor your real pattern.

Call 999 or go to a hospital emergency department if you have any of these, whatever your reading:

  • chest pain, or shortness of breath
  • a sudden, severe headache
  • weakness or numbness of the face, arm or leg on one side
  • trouble speaking, confusion, or a sudden change in your eyesight

If the reading is 180/110 or higher and you feel well, rest quietly for 5 minutes and measure again. If it is still 180/110 or higher, see a doctor today. If any of the signs above start, call 999.

If you are pregnant or gave birth in the last few weeks, do not wait. See a doctor without delay for any reading of 140/90 or higher. Go to a hospital emergency department straight away if you also have a severe headache, blurred vision or flashing lights, pain below the ribs, sudden swelling of the face, hands or feet, or vomiting.

What about low blood pressure?

Low blood pressure is a reading below 90/60. If you feel fine, it is usually nothing to worry about. Some people run low because they are fit and healthy, or because it runs in the family. See a doctor if you faint, if you keep feeling dizzy or generally weak, or if you take blood pressure tablets and your readings have dropped. Do not stop the tablets without asking your doctor. Call 999 if someone faints and cannot be woken up within 1 minute, or is confused, breathing very fast, or has blue, pale or blotchy skin.

If your reading is high

One high reading does not mean you have hypertension. The doctor confirms it with repeat readings on different days, or with your home readings. If it is confirmed, these changes help:

  • Less salt. Cut back on soy sauce, instant noodles, salted fish, processed meat and ready-made sauces.
  • Move. 30 minutes of brisk walking, five days a week.
  • Lose weight if you are overweight. Even 5 kg makes a difference.
  • Drink less alcohol.
  • Stop smoking. It may not lower the reading itself, but it lowers your overall risk of heart disease and stroke.

Many people also need medicine, and it works best taken every day, even when you feel well. The doctor also checks your blood sugar, cholesterol and kidneys, because they affect your heart risk.

Questions people ask

Is 130/80 a normal blood pressure?

Under the Malaysian guideline, a top number of 130 to 139 is in the "at risk" range. It is not hypertension yet, but it is a sign to cut salt, move more and check again within 3 to 6 months. If you have diabetes, kidney disease, heart disease or a past stroke, see your doctor about it, because you may need treatment at this level.

Is 140/90 high blood pressure?

Yes, if it is found on more than one day. A single reading of 140/90 needs to be checked again before the doctor calls it hypertension.

What is a normal blood pressure for a 60-year-old?

The same as for any adult: below 130/85 is normal, and 140/90 or higher on repeat readings is high blood pressure.

Can stress raise my blood pressure?

Yes, for a while. Stress, pain, coffee and rushing to the clinic can all push a reading up. This is why the doctor repeats readings and often asks you to measure at home.
