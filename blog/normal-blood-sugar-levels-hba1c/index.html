What is a normal blood sugar level? Fasting, random and HbA1c explained

Nearly one in six adults in Malaysia has diabetes, and more than a third of them do not know it. Here is how to read your numbers.

Permai Polyclinics FortunaJalan Penampang, Kota Kinabalu · 26 October 2026 · 9 min read
The short answer

For adults who are not pregnant, a normal blood sugar level is below 6.1 mmol/L after an overnight fast, and below 7.8 mmol/L when tested at any time without fasting. A normal HbA1c is below 5.7%. Under the Malaysian guideline, a fasting level of 7.0 mmol/L or higher, or an HbA1c of 6.3% or higher, means diabetes, and the numbers in between mean prediabetes. If you have no symptoms, the doctor needs two abnormal results before confirming diabetes.

The numbers, from the Malaysian guideline

A fasting blood sugar is taken after you have not eaten overnight. An oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) checks it again 2 hours after a drink containing 75 g of glucose. HbA1c shows your average blood sugar over the past 3 months, so you do not need to fast for it. Doctors in Malaysia use these cut-offs from the national guideline on type 2 diabetes:

CategoryFasting (mmol/L)HbA1c2 hours into OGTT (mmol/L)
Normalbelow 6.1below 5.7%below 7.8
Prediabetes6.1 to 6.95.7% to 6.2%7.8 to 11.0
Diabetes7.0 or higher6.3% or higher11.1 or higher

A random reading (taken any time without fasting) is normal below 7.8 mmol/L, and 11.1 or higher points to diabetes. With symptoms, one abnormal result is enough. Without symptoms, the doctor needs two: from one blood sample (such as blood sugar plus HbA1c) or from samples on different days.

HbA1c 6.3% or 6.5%: which cut-off does Malaysia use?

In the US, the CDC counts an HbA1c of 6.5% or above as diabetes. Malaysia uses 6.3%, because the Metabolic Syndrome Study of Malaysia (2009), which compared HbA1c with the OGTT in more than 4,000 adults, found that 6.3% gave the best balance of finding diabetes and avoiding false alarms across the country's three main ethnic groups. 6.5% found fewer people. So an HbA1c of 6.4% is under the US line, but here it is in the diabetes range, and if you have no symptoms it needs a second abnormal result. HbA1c can still miss diabetes: even at 6.3%, it picked up only about 4 in 10 of the new cases in that study. If your HbA1c or fasting level is in the prediabetes range, the doctor may suggest an OGTT to check further.

Do normal blood sugar levels change with age?

The cut-offs above are the same for adults of any age, unless you are pregnant. In pregnancy the limits are lower: diabetes in pregnancy is diagnosed at a fasting level of 5.1 mmol/L or higher, or 7.8 or higher 2 hours into an OGTT. HbA1c is not used to diagnose diabetes in teenagers under 18, and it may not give a true picture if you have anaemia, thalassaemia, advanced kidney disease or liver cirrhosis, or take iron tablets or steroids, so the doctor may use a blood sugar test instead. What changes with age and health is the treatment target once you have diabetes:

WhoHbA1c target
Newly diagnosed, younger and healthy, low risk of low sugar6.5% or below
Most adults with diabetes7.0% or below
Those with serious heart, kidney or liver disease, dementia, or frequent or severe lows7.1% to 8.0%
Older peopleDepends on health more than age: 7.5% or below if healthy, below 8.0% with several long-term illnesses, below 8.5% if in poor health or long-term care

At home, most people with diabetes aim for 4.4 to 7.0 mmol/L before meals, and 4.4 to 8.5 mmol/L at least 90 minutes after eating. Your doctor sets your own targets so your sugar does not drop too low.

Why so many people do not know they have diabetes

Up to half of people with diabetes have no symptoms. The National Health and Morbidity Survey 2023 found that 15.6% of Malaysian adults have diabetes, but only 9.7% know it. The other 5.9% have raised blood sugar without knowing, which is more than a third of everyone with diabetes. In October 2026 the Health Minister said over 80% of Malaysians aged 18 to 29 with diabetes or high blood pressure did not know it.

When symptoms do show, they include thirst, passing urine often (including at night), hunger, tiredness, blurred vision, weight loss without trying, and genital itching. If you have any of these, get a blood test soon. If you do not, still do not wait for symptoms. The guideline advises a blood test every year for:

  • every adult from age 30
  • anyone overweight (BMI 23 or more, or a waist of 80 cm or more in women and 90 cm or more in men) with one more risk factor, such as diabetes in a parent, brother or sister, high blood pressure, inactivity or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), even before 30
  • women who had diabetes in pregnancy
  • anyone with prediabetes

How to check your blood sugar at home

  1. Wash your hands with soap and warm water, and dry them well.
  2. For a fasting reading, test on waking, before eating or drinking anything but plain water.
  3. For an after-meal reading, test at least 90 minutes after eating.
  4. Write down each reading, the time and what you ate, and bring the list to the doctor.

A home meter is for keeping track. It cannot confirm diabetes. If you have not been diagnosed with diabetes and it reads 5.6 mmol/L or more when fasting, or 7.8 or more at any time, get a confirming lab test: fasting blood sugar, OGTT or HbA1c.

Call 999 or go to a hospital emergency department if your blood sugar is high, or you think it might be, and you have:

  • nausea, vomiting or stomach pain
  • faster breathing than usual, or a racing heartbeat
  • drowsiness, or trouble staying awake
  • confusion, or trouble concentrating
  • breath that smells fruity
  • a high ketone level in your blood or urine, if you test for it
  • passing out

These can be signs of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA, where ketones build up in the blood) or hyperglycaemic hyperosmolar state (HHS, very high sugar with severe dehydration). Both are life-threatening. Do not drive yourself. DKA can happen even when blood sugar is normal or only a little high in people taking an SGLT2 inhibitor (such as dapagliflozin or empagliflozin) and in pregnancy, so get the same emergency help if you have these signs.

If your reading is very high, for example 22.2 mmol/L (400 mg/dL), but you feel well, see a doctor today. If your reading stays at 16.7 mmol/L (300 mg/dL) or above, go to a hospital emergency department.

Call 999 as well if someone with low blood sugar becomes unconscious, has a fit or stops responding normally. Do not give them food or drink, because they cannot swallow safely. If they have a glucagon injection and you know how to use it, give it. Lay them on their side and stay with them.

What about low blood sugar?

Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia) is a reading below 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL). It happens mostly to people on insulin or tablets such as gliclazide. Signs include shaking, sweating, a pounding heart, hunger and confusion. Take 15 g of fast-acting sugar, such as 1 tablespoon of honey or 150 to 200 ml of fruit juice. If you take acarbose, use glucose tablets, or milk or honey if you have none. Check again after 15 minutes and repeat if still below 3.9, then eat your usual meal or snack. See a doctor if lows happen often, because your medicine may need to change, or if you get these signs and do not have diabetes.

If your reading is high

One high reading on a home meter does not confirm diabetes, but it does need a lab test. If you have prediabetes or diabetes, these changes can help bring blood sugar down:

  • Swap sweet drinks for plain water. That includes teh tarik, sweet kopi, air sirap and soft drinks.
  • Follow the Malaysian Healthy Plate: half vegetables and fruit, a quarter fish, chicken or beans, and a quarter rice, noodles or bread. Pick brown rice or wholemeal bread when you can.
  • Move. 30 minutes of brisk walking, five days a week. If you are older, have not been active, have heart disease or use insulin, ask your doctor first.
  • Lose weight if you are overweight: at least 7% of your weight with prediabetes, and 5% to 10% with diabetes.
  • Stop smoking.

On diabetes medicine: metformin is the first choice in the Malaysian guideline. If it does not suit you, for example because of kidney disease, the doctor will choose another medicine, and the doctor can add other medicines or insulin if you do not reach your target. Take your medicine as prescribed, even when you feel well, and do not stop it or change doses without asking the doctor. If you are ill with vomiting, diarrhoea or a fever, or plan to fast, ask your doctor or pharmacist what to do with your medicine.

Questions people ask

What is the normal HbA1c range?

Below 5.7% (39 mmol/mol) is normal. Under the Malaysian guideline, 5.7% to 6.2% is prediabetes and 6.3% or higher is diabetes.

What is a normal fasting blood sugar?

Below 6.1 mmol/L (110 mg/dL). A fasting level of 6.1 to 6.9 is prediabetes, and 7.0 mmol/L or higher is in the diabetes range. If you have no symptoms, a second abnormal result is needed to confirm it.

How long should I fast before a blood sugar test?

The Malaysian guideline asks for at least 8 hours. An HbA1c test needs no fasting. Follow the instructions from your doctor or lab.

What should I do if my blood sugar is 400?

400 mg/dL is 22.2 mmol/L, which is very high. If you are vomiting, breathing fast, drowsy or confused, call 999 or go to a hospital emergency department. If you feel well, see a doctor today, and go to the emergency department if the reading stays this high.
